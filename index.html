Purkin

Human, by choice.

A manifesto for keeping room, in an age of capable machines, for the things people do themselves and with each other.

Read ↓

The manifesto

  1. We are glad of the machines.

    Artificial intelligence will cure, build and teach things we could not manage alone. We use it. Nothing here asks anyone to stop.

  2. Worth is not a ranking.

    A person does not have to beat a machine to matter. Whatever was worth doing yourself is still worth doing when something else can do it faster, cheaper or better. You learn the song to be someone who plays it.

  3. Some things are the doing.

    A meal cooked for you. A letter written to you. An hour someone gives you. What you receive is that a person chose to. Hand it off and it becomes a different thing, however good.

  4. A choice needs somewhere to happen.

    When every screen, earpiece and counter has an assistant in it, the unassisted version stops being the default. It survives only where someone sets it up on purpose.

  5. So we set it up.

    Tables, rooms, evenings, workshops and works where people are the promise: made by a person, served by a person, in the company of people, with nothing in between.

  6. We say exactly what we promise.

    “Written by a person” means the words. “This room, after six” means that room, after six. We don’t claim purity, and we don’t hide the help we took.

  7. Everyone is free to come and go.

    Nothing is banned and nobody is recruited. The people who build AI are as welcome at the table as anyone, and so is whatever keeps you well: a hearing aid, a pump, a phone you’re on call with.

Begin tonight

Hold a Purkin evening.

You don’t need permission, a membership or a sticker. You need a table.

  1. Invite people, in person. Two is enough.
  2. Put the devices away. Off and out of reach, not face down. No assistants, earbuds or smart glasses.
  3. Do something together. Cook, play, read aloud, mend, make music, argue kindly.

Then tell them what it was, and that they can hold the next one.

Red

People only at the table. Devices come in, switched off and stowed. A dinner, a café courtyard, a class.

Purple

Nothing artificial takes part. Devices stay at the door. A reading room, a weekend, a house.

Two words, so a host can say in advance what a guest is walking into. Purple is the stricter one, as the dye was always the rarer. Both describe the evening, never the people.

Where this goes

Purkin is new. Today it is this page and whoever acts on it.

If enough tables fill, the next steps are plain: a short public standard, so cafés, makers and houses can make the same promise and be checked on it; and places built for the purpose, where you can stay a while.

The evenings come first. A mark is only worth something once people already care what it stands for.

The name comes from Purkinje cells, the great branching neurons of the cerebellum that Santiago Ramón y Cajal drew by hand, in ink, more than a century ago. They are part of how a body learns by practice. It’s a picture we like, not an argument.

Make room.

Hold an evening. Print this page and pin it where people meet. Add your name.
